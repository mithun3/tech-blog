# Virtual Machines

A virtual machine is a whole computer that happens to be a process inside another one. That trick is useful, and it is not free.

These pages cover how it works underneath, what it costs in practice, and the hypervisors and tools you are likely to meet on the way.

## In This Section

- [VM Architecture](/pages/virtualization/virtual-machines/vm-architecture) — how QEMU and KVM divide the work, what a guest really sees, and which defaults are worth keeping.
- [The Virtualisation Tax in Microseconds](/pages/virtualization/virtual-machines/virtualisation-latency) — how much latency does a hypervisor actually add? We put bare metal Rocky Linux up against a tuned and untuned Proxmox VM to find out.
- [VirtualBox](/pages/virtualization/virtual-machines/virtualbox) — Oracle's desktop hypervisor, where most homelab journeys start, and why most people eventually move on.
- [VMware ESXi](/pages/virtualization/virtual-machines/vmware-esxi) — the enterprise hypervisor that dominated data centres, what Broadcom did to it in 2024, and why many people moved to Proxmox.
- [XCP-ng](/pages/virtualization/virtual-machines/xcp-ng) — why XCP-ng is the closest direct Proxmox alternative for homelabs, where it feels stronger, and where Proxmox still wins.
- [Vagrant](/pages/virtualization/virtual-machines/vagrant) — repeatable, ephemeral VMs from a single file: the `vagrant up` and `vagrant destroy` workflow for development and experimentation.
