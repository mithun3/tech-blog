# Tailscale, And No Open Ports

DRAFT

Decided in September 2026, and tightened on 20 October 2026.

For a long time, reaching the lab from outside meant publishing something. A tunnel here, a reverse proxy there, and a login page sitting on the public internet hoping nobody interesting found it.

I do not do that any more.

I reach the lab through Tailscale. Nothing on the router answers the internet, and no port is forwarded.

## What I Did Before

The older pages publish services through a [Cloudflare Tunnel](/pages/networking/service-exposure/cloudflare-tunnel) or a [reverse proxy](/pages/networking/service-exposure/nginx-reverse-proxy-lxc). Both are good at what they are for: sharing something with people who cannot install a client.

For reaching my own admin pages, they were more exposure than I needed. A login page on the internet is still a page on the internet.

[Tailscale ZTNA & Subnet Routing](/pages/networking/homelab-network/tailscale-ztna) describes the first version of this design. This page is about the choices inside it.

## Three Ways To Do It

I looked at three.

| Option | For it | Against it |
|---|---|---|
| WireGuard on the router | Nothing outside the lab to depend on | A key and a firewall rule made by hand for every device, and the device key is the only credential |
| Headscale, a control server I host | The list of my devices stays at home | One more service to upgrade and keep reachable, and a trap: if it is down when my session expires, I cannot sign in to fix it |
| Tailscale, managed | Someone else runs the control server, and sign-in comes with it | A company is part of the path |

I chose the third.

The control server knows which devices I have. It does not carry my traffic. And if it goes down, devices that are already connected keep talking until a key expires. Only new devices have to wait.[^1]

So an outage on their side is not an outage on mine. Not straight away, at least.

## What I Chose

**Two routers that do the same job.** A subnet router is a device that acts as a gateway into a network for everything else on the tailnet.[^2] I run two: a container on Node 2 and a Raspberry Pi on the router's own port. Both advertise the same routes, which is how Tailscale provides failover.[^3] Either one can die without locking me out.

**Narrow routes.** The routers advertise the management networks and nothing else. Never the whole private range. A route that wide collides with other people's networks the first time I sit in one.

**Exit nodes for travel.** Both routers are also exit nodes, so on a network I do not trust I can send everything home first.[^4]

**Access for people, not for devices.** I sign in through an identity provider with passkeys. Device approval is on, so a new device cannot send or receive anything until I have approved it.[^5]

## The Router That Does One Job

The Raspberry Pi used to do more. It ran a small Kubernetes install as well, with no workload on it.

That sounds harmless. It is not. A router into the management networks is a security boundary, and everything else running on it widens what a break-in can reach.

An outage also happened on that board, and I never proved its cause.

So I took everything off it that was not routing and gave it a firewall that denies by default. It is a boring little machine now. That is the point.

## What I Turned Down

**Tailnet Lock.** It stops unknown devices too. But Tailscale's own page warns that if the disablement secrets are lost, and none was left with their support, the tailnet cannot be recovered.[^6] Device approval stops unknown devices without that failure waiting for me.

**A single sign-on server, dynamic DNS and a fallback tunnel.** All three were in the plan. All three came out. Each has a condition that brings it back, so the decision is not a mood.

| Removed | It comes back when |
|---|---|
| A single sign-on server | A second regular user arrives, or three or more apps need a login |
| Dynamic DNS | I build a service that accepts connections from outside |
| A Cloudflare Tunnel kept as a fallback | I publish something to the internet |

## What It Costs Me

- If both routers are down at once, there is no way in from outside. Recovery is on site, at the console.
- I depend on a company. Tailscale sees my device list and is part of how I sign in.

## When I Would Change My Mind

WireGuard stays reserved for a permanent tunnel between two sites, if there is ever a second site. Headscale stays on the shelf in case keeping the device list at home becomes a requirement.
