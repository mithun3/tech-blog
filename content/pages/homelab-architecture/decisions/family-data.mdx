# Family Data Gets Three Copies

DRAFT

Decided on 1 November 2026, and amended on 9 and 12 November 2026.

Everything else in the lab can be rebuilt. The photos cannot.

That is the whole reason this design is more careful than the rest of the lab.

<LabSheet sheet="services" focus="detail-b" caption="Detail B on sheet 3: NFS, shares and the pull." />

## What I Did Before

The storage started as a pool on the Proxmox host itself, which [Storage And ZFS](/pages/proxmox/operations/storage-and-zfs) still describes. Then it moved into a NAS VM. [TrueNAS SCALE On Proxmox](/pages/services/truenas-scale) and its three pages cover the build, the shares and the failure drills.

That gave the data a proper home. It was still one home.

## What I Chose

**The data lives once.** Photos, videos, documents, music projects and archives sit on one TrueNAS VM on Node 1, on a three-way mirror. The NAS runs no apps. It stores things.

**A second NAS pulls a copy.** An independent TrueNAS on Node 2, with its own three-way mirror, pulls a replica every night. It pulls. Nothing is pushed to it, so the primary holds no credential that could reach over and delete its own backups.

**A third copy leaves the house.** Two more drives, encrypted with a passphrase, rotate off site every month. No cloud, no monthly bill, and no new path to the internet from a network that is isolated on purpose.

**The apps get one VM of their own.** Immich, Jellyfin and Paperless-ngx run together in one Debian VM on Node 2, with a network card in the storage VLAN. Their databases stay on the VM's own disk. The files come from the NAS over NFS.

Not all of it is built yet. The first copy is live. The other two are still being built.

## Why A VM And Not A Container

Most small services in this lab are containers. This one is not.

Immich's own requirements say Docker inside LXC is not recommended.[^1] Proxmox's container guide still calls nesting containers inside a VM a recommended practice.[^2] When both projects point the same way, I stop arguing.

So the rule became: a VM for anything that owns storage or runs Docker against the NAS. A container for a single-process service.

## What I Turned Down

**Single-parity RAID for the backup pool.** Twice the usable space, but it survives only one drive failure, and a rebuild reads the whole pool for a day on drives that are no longer young. I took the mirror's safety margin.

**Installing the apps natively from helper scripts.** That route runs them in a container without Docker, on a setup the upstream projects do not ship. I wanted to run what the projects publish.

**A cloud copy instead of drives.** It stays on the list as the fallback if monthly rotation turns out to be more than I keep up with.

## What It Costs Me

- Each pool spends three drives to hold what one could. Mirrors are not the efficient choice.
- The off-site copy depends on me. A drive that does not get rotated is not a backup. It is a drive.
- There are two NAS systems to look after instead of one, and neither gets upgraded before a restore drill.

## When I Would Change My Mind

- When the pool gets close to full, both sides grow together: a second set of drives, or larger ones.
- If monthly rotation proves more than I keep up with, the cloud copy comes back onto the table.
