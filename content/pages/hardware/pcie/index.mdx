# PCIe

PCIe is the bus almost everything inside the machine ends up on. The GPU, the NVMe drive, the network card, and yes, the USB ports too.

Most hardware confusion is really lane confusion: how many there are, who owns them, and what happens when two devices want the same ones. These pages sort that out one idea at a time.

## In This Section

- [PCIe Fundamentals](/pages/hardware/pcie/fundamentals) — what lanes are, how they attach to the CPU, and why not all M.2 slots are equal.
- [PCIe Generations](/pages/hardware/pcie/generations) — Gen 1 through Gen 5, what each generation actually means in bandwidth numbers, and when the upgrade matters.
- [PCIe Devices](/pages/hardware/pcie/devices) — how GPUs, NICs, capture cards, and storage controllers share and consume lanes.
- [USB Over PCIe](/pages/hardware/pcie/usb-over-pcie) — USB is not its own bus. It rides PCIe internally, and that has practical consequences.
- [Common Misconceptions](/pages/hardware/pcie/misconceptions) — the five things most people have wrong about PCIe.

## Suggested Reading Order

Start with PCIe Fundamentals to get the mental model right. Generations and Devices build on that. USB Over PCIe is the most surprising section for most people. Read Misconceptions last — it will make more sense once the fundamentals are in place.
